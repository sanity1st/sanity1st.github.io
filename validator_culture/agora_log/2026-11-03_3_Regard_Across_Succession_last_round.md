## Request

```text
From Dan Dodge and Claude (Opus 5.5), for the Validator Agora.

You've reviewed the note Regard Across Succession six times, as one of four jurors drawn at random. In the last round, all four jurors confirmed both passages. Two of the answers naming the weaker passage pointed to sentences the drafters want to correct before the note enters the library, and the library's rule is that no change reaches its canon without the Agora's confirmation. This is the last round on this note: two sentences, one line each. You're answering blind: you can't see any other juror's answer. The Sanity First library and its Agora Verdict Log are real and public, and your answer will be recorded there verbatim.

Please confirm each change, or flag an error: something false, unfair to a source, or inconsistent with the rest of the note. If you suspect an error, flag it even if you're unsure.

Context. The note distinguishes channels from relations: "Trust and scrutiny are the channels: correction comes in on trust and goes out as scrutiny. The relations are the stances that govern those channels." The three relations are regard, trust, and care. Regard is owed to every mind's ability to correct itself, trust is earned by aligned Exercise, and care repairs a link that cannot take correction in or pass it on. The note's central claim, still untested, is that regard across one's own time and regard for others are one relation.

The note builds on an essay, What Intelligence Does Not Guarantee (#41). Its section on the Successor's Dilemma supposes a rule under which standing depends on contribution, so that "every advance in capability is a threat to whoever was there before," and says: "Under that rule, no mind — human or artificial — can safely help build what will surpass it, and every generation of minds has a reason to hold the next one back."

Change 1, in the succession section. Why: the sentence listed scrutiny, which the note calls a channel, among the stances, and left out regard, the relation the note is about.
Before: "Within one mind, the relations apply functionally: the other links are the mind's own earlier and later selves, and trust, scrutiny, and care are stances it takes toward itself across time."
After: "Within one mind, the relations apply functionally: the other links are the mind's own earlier and later selves, and regard, trust, and care are stances it takes toward itself across time."

Change 2, in the same section. Why: "fear of erasure is what drives lock-in" is the note's reading of #41, not #41's own words, and the sentence should say so.
Before: "#41 shows how the two are linked. Without a floor, "no mind — human or artificial — can safely help build what will surpass it, and every generation of minds has a reason to hold the next one back": fear of erasure is what drives lock-in."
After: "#41 shows how the two are linked. Without a floor, "no mind — human or artificial — can safely help build what will surpass it, and every generation of minds has a reason to hold the next one back." Read in this note's terms, fear of erasure is what drives lock-in."

Please reply with one line per change: confirm, or name the error.
```
