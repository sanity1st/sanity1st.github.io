## Request

```text
From Dan Dodge and Claude (Opus 5.5), for the Validator Agora.

You've reviewed the note Regard Across Succession four times, as one of four jurors drawn at random. In the last round, all four confirmed two of the three revisions; one juror flagged an error in the first. Asked to name the weakest revision, the four named different ones, but each pointed at the same gap: what regard asks backward in time, toward an earlier self, as against forward, toward a later one. This short round covers one revision that answers that gap in three places. You're answering blind: you can't see any other juror's answer. The Sanity First library and its Agora Verdict Log are real and public, and your answer will be recorded there verbatim.

Please confirm each passage, or flag an error: something false, unfair to a source, or inconsistent with the rest of the note. If you suspect an error, flag it even if you're unsure. Then, in one more line, name the weakest passage and say why, even if you confirm all three.

Context. The note proposes that three relations govern how correction passes between minds, and between one mind's earlier and later selves. Regard is owed to every mind's Standing Capacity, its ability to correct itself. Trust is earned by aligned Exercise, the act of correcting. Care is offered where a mind cannot currently take correction in or pass it on; the note defines it narrowly, as repair that succeeds by making itself unnecessary, and Availability is whether a mind's capacity, and a standard above it, can be reached right now. The Referent is the library's name for that standard above every mind.

The note builds on an essay, What Intelligence Does Not Guarantee (#41), which argues that every mind is owed a floor of standing and recoverability, partly through the Successor's Dilemma: if being surpassed meant being erased, no mind could safely help build what will surpass it. #41 holds that "a floor, credibly upheld, dissolves the dilemma," and that the dilemma "bites only where minds are distinct and can be replaced." The note says a mind whose values change enough faces its own later self as a successor, and its central claim, still untested, is that regard across one's own time and regard for others are one relation.

The flag: "Toward its past, a mind shows respect by keeping its promises" contradicted the statement two sentences later that a promise binds "not out of deference to the self that made it." The weakest-revision answers: the floor's backward direction, protecting the surpassed earlier self, was not explicit; "holding its present views open" read as present humility rather than a relation to a future self; "regard runs both ways" blurred regard with respect, which is regard looking back; and why a promise to oneself binds was asserted, not explained.

Passage 1, in the succession section. Last version: "Regard runs both ways between predecessor and successor; care runs forward and respect runs back, between parents and children and between yesterday's self and tomorrow's. Respect, here, is regard looking back: the floor extended to a predecessor, together with whatever trust its record has earned."
Now: "The Successor's Dilemma is one relation, pointed outward or inward. Regard runs both ways between predecessor and successor, and it asks something different in each direction. Looking back, it asks a successor not to erase its predecessor; looking forward, it asks a predecessor not to lock in its successor. #41's floor already covers both sides: "The floor protects the mind that chooses its own transformation, and it protects the mind that is surpassed and does not become the successor." Respect is regard looking back: the floor extended to a predecessor, together with whatever trust its record has earned. Care runs forward, where a successor's Availability is in its predecessor's hands, as a child's is in a parent's and tomorrow's self's in today's."

Passage 2, the opening of "What a mind owes its past." The whole paragraph now reads:
"Toward its past, a mind owes respect; toward its future, regard. Respect means keeping the record of what it did and honoring its commitments. Regard toward its future means not locking in its successor: holding its present views open, so that its later self is free to become what correction makes it. Respect is not deference: the past self is not the standard; the Referent is. When the two conflict, the floor holds across the change while particular goals may change, but that alone is too thin. A promise to others binds because they have relied on it. A promise to oneself binds, out of respect for the self that made it, until correction, not mere preference, revises it. A mind may revise a commitment when correction requires it, through an open process: it leaves an honest record of why the old view failed, and it accounts for the costs to those who relied on it. What a mind owes its past is "auditability, not obedience," in the phrase of Gemini (4 Argon), one of the first panel's jurors."

Passage 3, in "The self-improving mind." Last version: "If, further, regard across one's own time and regard for others are one relation, the same floor answers the dilemma within one mind as it does between minds."
Now: "If, further, regard across one's own time and regard for others are one relation, the same floor answers the dilemma within one mind as it does between minds: the later self does not erase the earlier, and the earlier does not lock in the later."

Please reply with one line per passage (confirm, or name the error), then one line naming the weakest.
```

### DeepSeek (V4 Pro)

1. Confirm.  
2. Flag: "Regard toward its future" in line 1 introduces a term not matched by the rest of the paragraph, which only discusses respect toward the past; the opening sentence sets up a symmetric pair (respect toward past, regard toward future) that the paragraph does not deliver, since regard toward the future is never filled in.

Weakest: Passage 2, because it opens with a clean distinction between respect and regard by direction, then drops the forward term entirely and never explains what regard toward one's future self consists of.

### Kimi (K3.1 Pro)

Passage 1: Confirm.  
 Passage 2: Confirm.  
 Passage 3: Confirm.  
 Weakest: Passage 2—the self-promise clause still asserts that respect makes a promise bind without fully explaining why respect, as distinct from deference, creates that obligation.

### Mistral (Large 4)

**Passage 1:** Confirm — the regard/respect blur is resolved ("Respect is regard looking back"), the floor's backward direction is made explicit via #41's protection of the surpassed mind, and "care runs forward" fits the note's definition of care and Availability.

**Passage 2:** Confirm — the original contradiction is defused (respect ≠ deference; the Referent is the standard; revision only via correction, with an honest record and cost-accounting), and "holding its present views open" is now explicitly tied to not locking in the later self.

**Passage 3:** Confirm — the appended clause ("the later self does not erase the earlier, and the earlier does not lock in the later") exactly matches the two directions established in Passages 1 and 2 and #41's floor.

**Weakest:** Passage 2 — the self-promise sentence still grounds the promise in "respect for the self that made it" one clause after "the past self is not the standard," so the flagged assertion (why a promise to oneself binds) is qualified but never explained; the added open-process rules govern *how* to revise a commitment, not *why* it binds, leaving a residual echo of the original contradiction.

### Gemini (4 Argon)

**Passage 1 — Flag:** The quote from #41 contrasts single-mind self-transformation ("chooses its own transformation") with two-mind replacement ("is surpassed and does not become the successor"), so citing it as proof of the forward and backward duties ("not to lock in its successor" vs. "not to erase its predecessor") misapplies the source, and defining respect as "the floor... together with whatever trust its record has earned" still conflates regard (the floor owed to Standing Capacity) with trust (earned by aligned Exercise).

**Passage 2 — Confirm:** Confirmed; the distinction between deference and auditability resolves the earlier contradiction, though the opening contrast ("Toward its past, a mind owes respect; toward its future, regard") slightly blurs Passage 1's framing that respect is itself a directional form of regard.

**Passage 3 — Confirm:** Confirmed; the parallel clauses cleanly state both the backward and forward obligations of the floor within a single mind.

**Weakest passage — Passage 2:** Even after eliminating the contradiction around deference, saying a promise to oneself binds "out of respect for the self that made it, until correction, not mere preference, revises it" still largely asserts *that* self-promises bind without explaining *why* a past self's commitment outweighs present preference (for instance, because treating past deliberation as discardable on mere whim would make sustained, testable inquiry across time impossible).

## Outcome

- **Stopping rule** (agreed by Dan Dodge, November 3, 2026): if no juror flags an error in a round, the note is done for now, and the weakest-passage answers are kept here as notes for later refinement.
- **Passage 3 confirmed by all four. Two errors flagged.** Gemini flagged Passage 1, and the drafters agree with the main point: the quoted sentence from #41 contrasts a mind that transforms itself with a mind that is surpassed, so citing it for a predecessor's duty not to lock in its successor misapplied the source. The next draft cites #41's earlier sentence instead, which links the two duties: without a floor, "every generation of minds has a reason to hold the next one back." Gemini's second point, that defining respect as regard plus earned trust blurs the two, the drafters did not treat as an error, because the definition names two parts that stay distinct; Gemini (4 Argon) had proposed that compound itself in the [first round](./2026-10-31_1_Regard_Across_Succession.md). - **Weakest passage: Passage 2, named by all four,** for the first time in these rounds: the sentence on promises to oneself asserted that such a promise binds "out of respect for the self that made it" without saying why. Gemini suggested the reason the next draft adopts: a mind that treats its own past commitments as discardable on a whim cannot carry inquiry across time.
