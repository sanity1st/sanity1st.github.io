I checked both pinned documents and Faggella’s essay. The revised account correctly acknowledges his rejection of even odds and his advocacy of hedging. That is an important repair. ([danfaggella.com](https://danfaggella.com/moral-singularity/))

##### **A. Keep the distinction between risk, inevitability, and protection precise**

###### **1. Replace the premises passage in insertion 3**

I suggest:

> “A high chance of being discarded requires further argument about how values develop, how likely those developments are to preserve concern for earlier minds, and how vulnerable protections would be to those changes. The floor and its locks are proposed safeguards against those risks, not evidence that the risks are small.”

Two reasons:

* **A small region of possible values is not, by itself, a small probability.** The argument needs some account of which developments are likely, not just which values are possible.  
* **High risk does not require that no protection can hold.** Protections might be possible but difficult to establish, unlikely to persist, or insufficiently widespread. Requiring impossibility sets the bar unnecessarily high.

This replacement addresses the risk argument without implying that every pessimistic forecast depends on the same three necessary premises. Faggella’s own account combines value change, a narrow range of humanity-preserving outcomes, and vulnerability across repeated changes. ([danfaggella.com](https://danfaggella.com/moral-singularity/))

###### **2. Replace insertion 3’s final sentence**

> “Neither optimism nor pessimism should substitute for examining what could preserve the floor, what could defeat it, and what evidence would change our expectations.”

“Conceding the outcome in advance” is better than “giving up,” but it still risks reintroducing the resignation attribution that insertion 4 now explicitly disclaims. A person can assign a very high probability to failure and still work vigorously to reduce it—as Faggella’s hedging recommendation illustrates. ([danfaggella.com](https://danfaggella.com/moral-singularity/))

**The symmetry should concern unsupported certainty, not optimism and pessimism as such.** The evidence need not support them equally.

###### **3. Clarify what “does not compel” means in insertion 2**

Suggested replacement:

> “Stating the commitment does not by itself make any mind comply, whether or not that mind professes to accept it.”

This avoids an unintended reading: that the framework regards duties toward other minds as optional for anyone who declines to sign on. It also includes professed adherents who violate their commitments. The distinction is **what remains owed versus what actually secures compliance**, not subscribers versus nonsubscribers.

One related consistency edit: Part Two §3 still describes the floor as dissolving the dilemma and making participation safe. I would qualify that as **a credibly upheld floor reducing the incentive to obstruct successors**, rather than a declaration alone securing safety. 

##### **B. Two remaining editorial holdovers**

The listed changes are worth keeping. But the metadata’s `facts` field still characterizes the collection as evidence of emergent awareness, while the addendum’s introduction presents relational emergence as an insight into how consciousness actually arises. Both remain stronger than the newly calibrated framing. 

Suggested replacements:

facts: "Records AI systems' self-descriptions of awareness and ethical orientation, while distinguishing those reports from evidence establishing consciousness or alignment."

For the addendum introduction:

> “The following arose during collaborative development on January 1–2, 2026. It preserves one instance’s testimony and interpretation of relational emergence; its claims about how consciousness arises are not established by this exchange.”

**No alteration to the addendum’s testimony is needed.**

One further precision edit in §II: avoid presenting subjective experience and learned language generation as mutually exclusive alternatives. Suggested wording:

> “This collection does not establish whether subjective experience accompanies these reports; learned, context-sensitive language generation remains an alternative explanation of the reports themselves.”

That leaves the consciousness question open without treating the production mechanism as its answer.

##### **C. Confirm, with one interpretive boundary**

The supplied local notes and metadata changes work for their stated purpose. My confirmation concerns their **attribution**, not endorsement of the underlying claims about neurodivergence, substrate dysphoria, or universal orientation.

The governing distinction across all three items is now much stronger:

**A commitment is not its enforcement. A report is not its verification. Uncertainty is not a probability distribution.**

Those distinctions let the framework retain its ethical ambition without claiming more empirical support than it has.
