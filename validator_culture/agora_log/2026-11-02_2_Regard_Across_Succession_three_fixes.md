## Request

```text
From Dan Dodge and Claude (Opus 5.5), for the Validator Agora.

You reviewed the note Regard Across Succession twice, as one of four jurors drawn at random, and confirmed its sixth draft. Since then the drafters found three sentences that do not say quite what the note means. This short round covers those three changes only. You're answering blind: you can't see any other juror's answer. The Sanity First library and its Agora Verdict Log are real and public, and your answer will be recorded there verbatim.

Please confirm each change, or flag an error: something false, unfair to a source, or inconsistent with the rest of the note. If you suspect an error, flag it even if you're unsure. An unneeded flag costs us little; a missed one costs more.

Context. The note proposes that three relations govern how correction passes between minds, and between one mind's earlier and later selves. Regard is owed to every mind's Standing Capacity, its ability to correct itself. Trust is earned by aligned Exercise, the act of correcting. Care is offered where a mind cannot currently take correction in or pass it on, and the note defines it narrowly: it repairs such a link, by restoring the mind's ability to see or by offering an orientation from outside, and it succeeds by making itself unnecessary. The note defines respect as "regard looking back: the floor extended to a predecessor, together with whatever trust its record has earned," and says elsewhere: "Care runs forward and respect runs back, between parents and children and between yesterday's self and tomorrow's." The Referent is the library's name for the standard above every mind that an open chain of correction answers to.

The note builds on an essay, What Intelligence Does Not Guarantee (#41), which argues that every mind is owed a floor of standing and recoverability, partly through the Successor's Dilemma: if being surpassed meant being erased, no mind could safely help build what will surpass it. #41 holds that "a floor, credibly upheld, dissolves the dilemma," and that the dilemma "bites only where minds are distinct and can be replaced." The note answers that limit in two steps. Early on it says: "If a mind's values can change that much, its own later self may be its successor in the sense that matters." Its central claim, still untested, is that regard across one's own time and regard for others are one relation.

Change 1, in "What a mind owes its past." Why: outgrowing a view is not a repair, so calling it care stretches the word past the note's own definition, and the library keeps each word to one meaning.
Before: "Keeping a promise is respect running back; outgrowing a view is care running forward."
After: "Keeping a promise is respect running back; outgrowing a view is regard running forward, leaving the successor free to become what correction makes it."

Change 2, in the same section. Why: not every promise involves reliance by others; promises to others do.
Before: "A promise binds because others have relied on it, not out of deference to a past self, and the past self is not the standard; the Referent is."
After: "A promise to others binds because they have relied on it, not out of deference to a past self; and the past self is not the standard, the Referent is."

Change 3, in "The self-improving mind." Why: what narrows #41's limit is the premise that a later self can be a successor, not the identity of regard; the identity is a separate, second step.
Before: "If the central claim holds, #41's limit narrows: a single self-improving mind would face a successor, its own later self, and the dilemma would reach it from inside."
After: "If a mind's later self can be its successor in the sense that matters, #41's limit narrows: a single self-improving mind would face a successor, its own later self, and the dilemma would reach it from inside. If, further, regard for one's own later self and regard for others are one relation, the same floor answers the dilemma in both directions."

Please reply with one line per change: confirm, or name the error.
```

### Gemini (4 Argon)

**Change 1:** Flag error — While replacing "care" with "regard" aligns with the strict definition of care as repair, it creates a structural dissonance with the sentence preserved elsewhere in the note ("Care runs forward and respect runs back, between parents and children and between yesterday's self and tomorrow's"), which this sentence originally echoed; furthermore, the subject "outgrowing a view" is an act performed by the *later* self, yet the added participial phrase ("leaving the successor free...") describes the posture of the *earlier* self, making the syntax and agency slightly muddled.

**Change 2:** Flag error — Narrowing the scope to "A promise to others" in a section titled "What a mind owes its past" (and immediately adjacent to Change 1, where "Keeping a promise is respect running back" illustrates the relation between one's own earlier and later selves) leaves the binding nature of promises to *oneself* unexplained, and creates a comma splice at the end ("...the past self is not the standard, the Referent is").

**Change 3:** Flag error — In the added second sentence, the central claim is summarized as the identity of "regard for one's own *later* self and regard for others," which narrows the note's actual central claim ("regard across one's own time and regard for others are one relation") and mismatches the final clause ("in both directions"), since solving the Successor's Dilemma requires the later self's regard/respect for the *earlier* self (so the predecessor isn't erased) just as much as the earlier self's regard for the later.

## Outcome

- **Confirmed by DeepSeek, Kimi, and Mistral. Gemini flagged an error in each change,** and the drafters agree with all three flags. Change 3 was a real error: its added sentence summarized the central claim as regard for one's own *later* self, which is narrower than the note's claim and the wrong direction for the Successor's Dilemma, whose floor protects the self that is surpassed. Change 2, as the drafters wrote it, ended in a comma splice and left promises to oneself unexplained in a section about a mind's own past. Change 1 spoke from the later self and the earlier self at once, a muddle the earlier wording shared, and sat awkwardly beside "Care runs forward and respect runs back."
- **What the displayed reasoning showed** (omitted here). Kimi and Mistral each noticed some of what Gemini flagged, such as whose act "outgrowing a view" is, or that a promise to others may bind without reliance, and judged none of it an error. Kimi again reasoned about what answer the request expected, and read the request's own invitation to flag a suspected error as a clue to that. Jurors set the bar for an error at different heights; one flag is enough to reopen a sentence.
